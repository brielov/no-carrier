# The story

**Spoilers.** NO CARRIER is meant to be discovered by ear, a fragment at a
time, over many nights. If you would rather find it that way, stop here.

## In one line

Home went silent. You keep the lights on in a small relay capsule at the edge
of everything, listening for anyone at all, and slowly learn that the ship you
came out with found someone else who never stopped listening either.

## The heart of it

This is not a story about what happened to Earth, about getting home or being
rescued. It is about the after: being alone, not knowing whether anyone else is
left, and listening anyway. Searching is the purpose. The machines do not need
to understand the universe to keep doing their work, and neither do you.

That is why the receiver never stops scanning, and why the Meridian staying
with the Lighthouse is the happy ending: in all that silence, they found
someone else who had kept the lights on, and kept them company.

## The premise

The **Halcyon** and the **Meridian** left home together. The Meridian was a
survey ship with a crew of four, sent towards a faint, patient signal that no
one could explain. The Halcyon is its relay tender: a one-person capsule meant
to hold position at the last relay point and pass the Meridian's reports home.

On mission day 3,690, home stopped answering. All of it, at once. No one on
the air ever says why, and no one ever finds out. Mission control's last
message asked the Halcyon to keep the receiver running. **Relay Lima**, the
automated station behind you, still counts the days since, and still says "no
traffic" on schedule, because no one is left to tell it otherwise.

The Meridian went on anyway, into a region the crew call **the Quiet**, where
radio goes soft and slow. Its reports grew rarer, then stopped.

You are the Halcyon's operator, the one who stayed. Your job was to listen, so
you still do. You may be among the last people anywhere. You do not know.

What reaches you now is old, and out of order. The Meridian's broadcasts left
it about a year before you hear them. And very rarely, something faint comes up
from Earth itself: a voice, or an automated station. You cannot tell whether it
is live or an old recording still going round, and you never will.

## What happened, in order

The listener never hears it in this order. Day numbers are the mission clock,
counted from launch, and they are the thread an attentive listener can pull.

1. **Day 3,690.** Earth stops answering. Mission control's last message: "We
   may lose the link for a while. Whatever happens, keep the receiver running."
   The Meridian's crew decide to keep the schedule anyway.
2. **Day 3,902.** The Meridian crosses into the Quiet. Radio turns soft, "like
   talking under a blanket".
3. **Days 3,920 to 3,990.** The signal grows stronger. Sato finds it repeats
   every 41 minutes but never exactly the same, and that its message is simple:
   *we are here, we are still here*.
4. **Day 4,009.** They find the source: a relay, like the Halcyon. Very old,
   from an expedition nobody remembers, still running, with its lamps on and
   plants still growing under them. Sato names it **the Lighthouse**.
5. **Day 4,011.** The crew decide to stay with it, because someone should. They
   start sending the Lighthouse's message along with their own.
6. **Day 4,012.** The last dated message: nothing to report, lamps on, left on
   for you.
7. **Afterwards.** The beacon keeps looping. You keep listening. Now and then,
   something faint from the ground.

The Halcyon's own clock is later than all of this, and keeps counting while
you listen.

## The people

- **Commander Ruth Adair.** Steady, warm and dry. Her logs are short and kind.
  She is the one who says it is all right.
- **Dr. Lena Sato,** survey scientist. Curious and gentle. She finds the
  signal comforting before she understands it.
- **Tomas Vey,** engineer. Keeps everything running and complains about the fan
  bearings. Funny because things are fine, not because they are not.
- **Ezra Hale,** radio operator and the youngest. He talks to the Halcyon
  directly, as if to a friend. He also keys Morse, and he sends his dahs a
  little too long.
- **Mission control.** Heard only in old recordings in the Halcyon's archive,
  from before the silence. The last of them is the instruction you are still
  following.
- **Relay Lima.** Home's automated station. It counts the days since contact
  and never says anything new.
- **The ground.** Rare, faint and unexplained: an automated relay on the
  Pacific, a shelter station's beacon, and **Wren**, someone on an old radio
  with a greenhouse, calling anyone at all. They may be live. They may be years
  old. They are never in danger and never ask to be saved.
- **You.** Never named and never heard. The Halcyon's log is your voice.

## Rules of tone

- **The cause is never told.** No war, no plague, no ruins, no burning sky.
  Only the after: the silence, and the people still keeping things running.
- **Calm, never scary.** No distress calls, no danger, no deaths on air, no
  grief spoken aloud. Where something is unknown, it is wonder, not threat.
- **Hope is possible, never confirmed.** The ground voices might mean others
  survived, or might be echoes. Nobody finds out.
- **The computer is plain, not cheerful.** Reports state facts and stop.
- **Mostly ordinary.** For every line that moves the story, several are just
  life: pancakes, plants, a fan bearing, a quiet Sunday. That is what lets the
  story blend into the background.
- **Never resolved on air.** No message explains everything. The pieces fit
  only for someone who keeps listening.
- **No urgency.** Nobody needs rescuing. The question is not "will they be
  saved" but "is anyone there, and what are we to each other".

## How it reaches the listener

| Channel | What it carries |
| --- | --- |
| **Broadcasts** | The crew's recorded messages, the Meridian's beacon, mission control's old recordings, Relay Lima, and very rarely the ground. Heard often partway through, through fading. |
| **Morse** | Ezra's traffic, keyed by hand with his long dahs, including calls to Earth, plus other stations' meaningless keying. The words never appear on screen. |
| **The daily report** | Printed each time the Halcyon comes up: mission day, days since last contact with home (always climbing), communication attempts (always climbing), successful contacts (always 0), unknown signals (1), transmissions logged. Plain, without cheer. |
| **The system log** | The Halcyon's side: its day count, Relay Lima's silence, small operator notes, and how many transmissions you have logged. |
| **The machines** | Routine. The fan Vey keeps fixing is the same kind as yours. The hull ticks and settles as it warms and cools. |

## Writing more

New transmissions are welcome. Keep each one short (one to three sentences),
in one character's voice, and true to the rules above. Give a day number only
when the message is part of the timeline, and make sure it fits the order
above. Most new lines should be ordinary life, and nothing should say what
happened.
